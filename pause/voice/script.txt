Every day, the world freezes for ten minutes. Everyone except you. No traffic. No noise. A pigeon hangs in the air like a sticker. On day one, you skip the line at the coffee shop. Nobody notices. By day ten, you finish your homework. Then your neighbour's. By day one hundred, you have read a whole library, while the pigeon watches. By year ten, you know every frozen stranger in town. Then one morning, you look in the mirror. Your hair is greyer than your friends'. Luckily, you gain sixty extra hours every year. Unluckily, you are the only one who ages during them. Here is the math. Ten minutes a day is sixty hours a year. Over eighty years, that is about two hundred days. Everyone else lives eighty years. You live eighty years and half a year more. You are always a little older than your friends. A little wiser. A little more tired. And nobody can ever explain why. So at the end, they ask for your secret. You smile and say: ten minutes. Then you do what you always do with them. You close your eyes. And the whole world waits for you.
