5The four strokes7.2 – 11.4 s

On-screen text
1 INTAKE   2 COMPRESSION   3 POWER   4 EXHAUST (four chips, the current one filled)
What moves
Engine restarts slowly. Intake: the intake valve opens and pale blue air-fuel mix fills the chamber as the piston moves away. Compression: both valves shut, the mix is squeezed. Power: a white spark flash at the plug, the gas turns orange and pushes the piston. Exhaust: the exhaust valve opens, grey gas is pushed out. Chips follow the real crank angle.
Camera
Close. Focus drifts from the head along the cylinder to the crank.
Sounds
Intake rush. Rising squeeze tone. Tick, thud and a short bang on the spark. Exhaust puff. A valve click each time one opens. Flywheel whirr rises with speed.

What I assumed

  • "Normal engine" means a car (petrol, four-stroke) engine. If you meant diesel, a motorbike or a V8, say so.
  • One cylinder, lying on its side so the parts pull apart along a horizontal axis. A real car engine is upright and has four or more cylinders; the single one shows the whole idea.
  • Four strokes take two turns of the crank, so there is one power stroke for every two turns. That is the closing fact.
  • Gas colours are teaching colours: pale blue = air and fuel, orange = burning, grey = exhaust. Fuel injector, camshaft, oil pan, crankcase and timing belt are left out; the valves just open and close on the right strokes.
  • Valves and valve springs are one part. The spark plug is its own part that lifts off the head. Cylinder has cooling fins on its far end and a clear wall so the gas shows.
  • Proportions are for looks: crank radius 0.8, con-rod 4.0 (relative units), stroke 1.6. 13 s, English captions, no voiceover, no music.
