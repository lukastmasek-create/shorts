What moves
Engine restarts slowly. White steam fills the chamber behind the piston and pushes it along; at the end of the stroke the steam moves to the other side. Rod and crosshead slide, the con-rod swings, the crank turns. Caption 1 at 7.2 s, caption 2 at 8.8 s.

What I assumed

  • One horizontal, single-cylinder, double-acting engine. Steam pushes the piston both ways, so each stroke is half a turn of the crank. That is the "half a turn" fact on the last beat.
  • Shape is a teaching model, not a particular real engine. Proportions: crank radius 0.8, con-rod 3.5, piston rod 3.7 (relative units). Stroke is therefore 1.6.
  • Steam chest is drawn closed: the slide valve inside is not shown, and no boiler is drawn. Steam is shown as white puffs on the pushing side only. Pipe on the chest stands for the steam inlet, the small brass stub for the exhaust.
  • Cylinder wall is clear so the piston and steam can be seen. A real cylinder is cast iron. Flywheel is orange (the one moulded colour), crosshead carbide grey with orange guide shoes.
  • Seven parts, in order of the path of the force: cylinder, piston, piston rod, crosshead, connecting rod, crank, flywheel. Crank shaft bearings, governor and frame are left out.
  • Length 12 s. Captions are English, mono, large enough for a phone. No voiceover; music not used, only effects and a quiet room bed.
