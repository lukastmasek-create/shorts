Facts and numbers I had to assume

  • 180 °C as the popping temperature (about 356 °F) and about 9 atmospheres (about 135 psi) of steam pressure inside the hull: the commonly cited figures for popcorn. Real kernels vary a little (roughly 175 to 180 °C).
  • “Up to 40× bigger”: the cited volume expansion is 40 to 50 times; I used the cautious end and the words “up to”.
  • The blue drop is a symbol for the water in the kernel (about 14% of its weight), not drawn to scale. The corn beads and water beads stay the same two populations through the whole film: gold stays gold, blue boils off into white steam, steam leaves the frame at the burst.
  • The popped shape is one fat flake with two amber hull shards caught in its creases. A real flake is irregular, and much of the hull stays attached, so the shards are a hint, not a model.
  • Length 12 s, five beats, three seconds or less per caption, 52 px captions (about 5% of frame height) so they read on a phone. The beads never spell words or numbers.
  • Sound is a full mix without voiceover: glass clicks, burner hum, sizzle, hull creaks, one crack and a soft chime. No music, only a quiet room bed.
  • The stills use the real bead engine, not a simplified block-out, so the build will reuse this exact scene code and only add the player and sound.
