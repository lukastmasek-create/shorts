# Title: The Moon Never Turns Its Back

**Concept:** Why we only ever see one face of the Moon: it spins exactly once per orbit, because Earth's gravity slowed it down long ago (tidal locking). Pure geometry, one dot on a circle.

Format: English, one steady calm voice (~2.4 words/s), no on-screen captions (labels/pills only, as in STYLE.md), 1080x1920, 30 fps, ~37 s = ~1110 f. White line art on black, `SceneFx` transitions, full-canvas backdrops.

## Full narration (~92 words)
Look up at the Moon tonight. Same face. Same craters. Same dark seas. Every night of your life.
Is it standing still? No. It orbits Earth, and it also spins.
Here is the trick. One spin takes exactly as long as one orbit. Twenty-seven days, both.
So as it travels around us, the same side keeps pointing at Earth. Like a dancer who never turns away from her partner.
It was not always this way. Billions of years ago, the Moon spun fast.
But Earth's gravity pulled on it, stretched it a little, and that tug acted like a brake.
Slower, and slower, until the spin matched the orbit. Then it locked.
So the far side stays hidden from us. Half of the Moon, we have never seen from home.

## Notes
- Facts: sidereal rotation and orbit are both ~27.3 days (say "twenty-seven"); tidal locking from Earth-induced tidal bulge + friction. Libration lets us see ~59% overall, so "never seen from home" refers to the far hemisphere (about 41%); keep the wording "half" loose or change beat 8 to "Almost half of the Moon" if fact-checking strictly.
- Visual keystone: one dot on the Moon that always faces Earth (beats 3, 4, 5, 7).
- All shapes are circles, arcs, dashed rings, arrows, a lock icon, pills: easy SVG with existing kit/kit2.
