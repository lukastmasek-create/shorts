Title: What If You Fell Through a Wormhole? #Shorts

Description:
One trip, Earth to Andromeda, about 2.5 million light-years, by wormhole. In 1935 Einstein and Rosen found a "bridge" in general relativity; in 1957 John Wheeler (with Charles Misner) gave it the name wormhole. In 1962 Fuller and Wheeler showed that bridge pinches shut faster than light can cross. In 1988 Morris and Thorne described one that could in principle stay open, held by exotic matter with negative energy: real (the Casimir effect) but tiny. From outside it would look like a sphere showing a distorted view of the far side, as rendered for Interstellar. The same math could make a time machine; Hawking (1992) conjectured physics forbids that. In 2022 a quantum chip ran a simulation of wormhole-like dynamics; no real tunnel in space was made. No wormhole has ever been observed; this is a what-if.

Sources: Einstein & Rosen, Phys. Rev. (1935); Misner & Wheeler, Annals of Physics (1957); Fuller & Wheeler, Phys. Rev. (1962); Morris & Thorne, Am. J. Phys. (1988); Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever, PRL (1988); Hawking, Phys. Rev. D (1992); Lamoreaux, PRL (1997); James et al., Am. J. Phys. (2015); Jafferis et al., Nature (2022); Hubble/STScI (2025).
