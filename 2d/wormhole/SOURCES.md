# WormholeShort (#8 "What If You Fell Through a Wormhole?") — fact check
Checked by web search 2026-11-15 (all URLs accessed 2026-11-15). Most claims rest on primary-paper metadata/abstracts seen via search; full texts were not opened. Rows marked (secondary) rest on press/popular coverage. The whole premise ("you fall through and come out millions of light-years away") is hypothetical: say "if", never "would" as fact. Avoid overlap with the blackhole short (spaghettification, time slowing, event horizon "point of no return").

## Not verified / do not use
- "A Gao–Jafferis–Wall wormhole is slower than going the long way around" — widely repeated (attributed to Quanta/Jafferis) but not confirmed in what I could see. Do not claim.
- "Physicists created a wormhole" / "sent a message through a wormhole" (2022) — false framing.
- Any specific amount of exotic matter (e.g. "a Jupiter mass of negative energy") — numbers vary by model; not checked.
- Hermann Weyl 1921/1924/1928 wormhole idea — dates conflict across sources.
- Triangulum as farthest naked-eye galaxy (~2.7–3 Mly) — disputed, skip.
- "Wheeler coined it alone in 1957" — first print use is the Misner–Wheeler paper; say "named by John Wheeler" only.
- Tidal forces inside a traversable wormhole — Morris & Thorne designed theirs to be gentle on travellers (per abstract-level summaries only); don't claim you'd be stretched, and don't repeat the blackhole short's spaghetti.
- That exiting a wormhole would look like a "crystal ball reflecting a galaxy" — scraped-site wording; use "a sphere showing a distorted view of the other side" (Space.com/DNEG).
- Wormholes "exist inside black holes" / black holes as wormhole entrances — Morris & Thorne say no for travel.
