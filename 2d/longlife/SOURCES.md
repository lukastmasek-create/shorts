# LongLifeShort (#56 "Humans Evolved to Live for 1,000 Years") — fact check
Checked by web search 2026-11-16. Several hits were secondary (wikis, press); primary papers named so they can be opened. Arithmetic lines are marked "illustration".

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Longest verified human life: Jeanne Calment (France), 21 Feb 1875 – 4 Aug 1997, 122 years 164 days | Gerontology Research Group page https://www.grg-supercentenarians.org/jeanne-calment/ ; Guinness World Records https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/oldest-person | "verified" = documented record; her age has been questioned by a few researchers, never overturned officially |
| Global life expectancy at birth ~32 years in 1900, ~73 in 2023 | Our World in Data https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/life-expectancy-globally-since-1770 (HMD + UN WPP 2024) | averages, not maximum lifespan; 1800 figure NOT verified, not used |
| Human cells stop dividing after roughly 50 divisions (Hayflick limit); shortening telomeres are the later-found cause | Hayflick & Moorhead, Exp Cell Res 25:585–621, 1961; review Shay & Wright, Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol 2000 | count varies by source (40–60); say "around 50"; fetal fibroblasts in a dish, not whole body |
| Greenland shark: largest of 28 sharks dated at 392 ± 120 years (eye-lens radiocarbon); longest-lived vertebrate known | Nielsen et al., Science 353:702–704, 2016, DOI 10.1126/science.aaf1703 | estimate with wide error, say "about 400 years, give or take 120"; sexual maturity ≥156 ± 22 yrs |
| Hydra: in a 4-year study, death rate did not rise with age (negligible senescence) | summarised by lifespan.io https://www.lifespan.io/topic/is-immortality-possible/ ; Martínez, Exp Gerontol 33:217–225, 1998 | secondary summary opened; still dies of accidents/predation; lab study |
| 2016: Dong, Milholland & Vijg argued maximum human lifespan is fixed, ceiling ~115 avg, absolute ~125 | Nature 538:257–259, 2016, DOI 10.1038/nature19793 | contested (criticised in published replies); one side of a debate |
| 2021: blood-marker trends extrapolated to a loss-of-resilience point at 120–150 years | Pyrkov et al., Nature Communications 12:2765, 2021, DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-23014-1 | extrapolation, not observed limit; authors from biotech company Gero |
| 1,000 years = about 8 Calments (1000 / 122.4 = 8.2); 1,000 years ago = year 1026 | arithmetic | illustration |

## Do NOT claim
- Greenland shark "272 years" as a fixed age; 392 is a midpoint with ±120 error.
- A hard human limit of 125 or 150 as settled fact: both are published arguments, not proven.
- Anything that "humans evolved" to live 1,000 years or how fast evolution could do it; no source. Scenario parts are labelled "imagine".
- Biblical / legendary ages (Methuselah etc.) as data.
- Any social consequence of 1,000-year lives (population, wealth, politics) as prediction: pure speculation, only phrased as question.
- Life expectancy ~30 in 1800 (not verified here).
