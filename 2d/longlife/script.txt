What if humans lived for a thousand years? Not ninety. A thousand.
The global average life is about 73 years. The longest verified one is Jeanne Calment's: 122 years.
A thousand years is about eight of her lives, back to back.
In a dish, human cells stop dividing after around fifty divisions. Telomeres shorten every time.
In 2016, one team argued the ceiling is about 125. A 2021 projection said 120 to 150.
Nature sets no single rule. A Greenland shark was dated at about 400 years, give or take 120.
A tiny animal called hydra showed no rise in deaths with age, over four years.
Still, no human is close. For us, a thousand years is a question, not a forecast.
A thousand years ago was the year 1026. Imagine one life, reaching back that far.
