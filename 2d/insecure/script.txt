What if you could see everyone's insecurities, floating right above their heads?
You would see them on the winners too. In 1978, psychologists described top achievers who secretly felt like frauds. They called it the impostor phenomenon.
And everyone thinks their flaw is on display. In 2000, students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room. They guessed half the room noticed. Fewer than one in four did.
Nerves hide well too. In 2003, nervous speakers rated how anxious they looked. The audience saw less.
After talking to a stranger, people underestimate how much they were liked. It's called the liking gap. With new roommates, it lasted for months.
Even showing a flaw looks different from outside. In 2018, people judged their own vulnerability as weakness, and the same move by others as courage.
So if you could see everyone's insecurities, you'd learn one thing. Nobody is watching yours as closely as you are.
