# DeleteShort (#28 "Delete Embarrassing Memories") — fact check
Checked by web search 2026-11-14; most pages were secondary. Bars, gauge, curves are schematic (no values claimed).

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| People learned word pairs, practiced not thinking of some, later remembered those less | Anderson & Green, "Suppressing unwanted memories by executive control", Nature 410:366–369, 2001 (think/no-think) | word pairs, NOT embarrassing real-life memories; effect after ~10–16 suppression attempts |
| Propranolol given during fear-memory reactivation reduced fear response a day later; some later studies failed to repeat | Kindt, Soeter & Vervliet, Nature Neuroscience 12:256–258, 2009; failed/qualified replications: Chalkia et al. 2019, Bos et al. 2014, Schroyens et al. 2017 | fear conditioning with mild shock, not embarrassment; not a treatment claim |
| Negative feelings fade faster than positive (fading affect bias) | Walker, Skowronski & Thompson, 2003; Walker & Skowronski review, Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2009 | affect fades, memory content stays |
| Memory is not a recording; rebuilt on recall inside a system tied to the self | Conway & Pleydell-Pearce, "The construction of autobiographical memories in the self-memory system", Psychological Review 107(2):261–288, 2000 | |
| "Delete enough and you edit who you are" | inference from the self-memory model (memories tied to identity) | marked on screen as inference; no study deletes memories in people |
