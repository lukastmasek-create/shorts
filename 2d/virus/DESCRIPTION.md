Title: What If You Shrank to the Size of a Virus? #Shorts

Description:
Shrink about seventeen million times, to roughly 100 nanometres, the size of the coronavirus behind COVID-19. A red blood cell becomes a wall about 75 times wider than you, a bacterium is 20 times your length, and ordinary human cells are 100 to 300 times your width. Then see how a virus gets in: spikes dock onto ACE2, it makes the cell copy it, and one infected cell can release hundreds of new copies. (Size ratios are illustrations based on typical sizes and an assumed 1.7 m adult.)

Sources: CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases 27(4); Bar-On et al., eLife (2020); Sender, Fuchs & Milo, PLoS Biology (2016); HHMI; Scientific American, "Inside the coronavirus".
