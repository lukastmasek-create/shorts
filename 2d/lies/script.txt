What if you could see lies as colors? Truth glows green. Lies glow red.
Right now, you can't. In a meta-analysis of 206 research papers and over twenty four thousand judges, people spotted lies about fifty four percent of the time. Pure chance is fifty.
Experts did no better.
Why? Researchers checked one hundred fifty eight possible cues. Most were unrelated to lying, or only weakly linked. Nervousness was one of the few that showed a link, and it was small.
Even machines struggle. A 2003 report from the US National Research Council found that polygraphs beat chance, but fall well short of perfect.
So what would your colors show? Not a world of liars. In a survey of a thousand Americans, about sixty percent said they told no lies that day. A small group, about five percent, told almost half of all lies. By their own reports.
Your world would be mostly green, with a few people glowing red.
The scary part is not how many lies there are. It is how little we can tell.
