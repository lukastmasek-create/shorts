What if the Sun vanished for twenty-four hours?
For the first eight minutes, nothing happens. Sunlight takes eight minutes to reach us, so the sky stays bright.
Gravity travels just as fast, so Earth keeps orbiting as if nothing changed.
Then the light goes out. Day turns to night, everywhere at once.
The Moon and the planets go dark too. They only shine with borrowed sunlight.
Gravity lets go. Earth flies off in a straight line, thirty kilometers every second.
Plants stop making food. But the air and the oceans hold heat, so the cold comes slowly.
After one full day, the surface has cooled only a few degrees.
The real freeze would take weeks, and the oceans would stay liquid for far longer.
In those twenty-four hours, Earth drifts two and a half million kilometers off course.
Then the Sun returns, and gravity catches us again.
The dark is fast. The cold is slow.
