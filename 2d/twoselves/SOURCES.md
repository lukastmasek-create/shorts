# TwoSelvesShort (#29 "Two Personalities That Could See Each Other") — fact check
Checked by web search 2026-11-14; mostly secondary pages. Diagrams are schematic.

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Inner speech can work like a conversation with yourself | Alderson-Day & Fernyhough, "Inner speech: development, cognitive functions, phenomenology, and neurobiology", Psychological Bulletin 141(5):931–965, 2015 (reviews dialogic inner speech) | later fMRI work: dialogue engages theory-of-mind regions (cited in a 2023 review, not used) |
| People who felt less connected to their future self preferred smaller rewards now | Ersner-Hershfield, Wimmer & Knutson, "Saving for the future self", Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience 4(1):85–92, 2009 (rACC activity, current vs future self, predicted temporal discounting a week later) | correlational, individual differences |
| After the corpus callosum was cut each hemisphere acted on its own; the talking (left) side invented reasons for actions it did not choose | Sperry & Gazzaniga split-brain research (Sperry Nobel Prize 1981); summarised in BrainFacts | later work questions parts of the original conclusions; surgery now very rare |
| DID = two or more distinct identity states plus memory gaps beyond ordinary forgetting; recognized disorder | DSM-5 criteria (via secondary summaries) | prevalence not stated in video (≈1.5% 12-month in Johnson et al. 2006; other estimates lower) |
| "You do not have two selves watching each other" | contrast with DID: gaps, not mutual observation | framing |
