What if robots started dreaming? In a way, they already do.
In 1994, scientists recorded sleeping rats. Their brain cells replayed the maze they had run.
In 1995, Geoffrey Hinton's team gave a network a sleep phase. Awake, it learns. Asleep, it practices on made-up samples.
In 2015, Google's DeepDream let a network find shapes in noise, then amplify them.
In 2018, an agent learned a video game entirely inside its own imagined world. Then it played the real one.
In 2022, sleep-like phases helped networks recover old skills they had forgotten.
In 2025, DreamerV3 imagined the future to learn Minecraft, and found diamonds without human examples.
But do machines feel anything? There is no evidence. It is practice, not experience.
So, dreaming robots? Not yet. Practicing ones? Already here.
