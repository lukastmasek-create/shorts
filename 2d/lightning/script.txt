What if lightning hit you every single day? Not once in your life. Every day, for a year.
One bolt can heat the air to fifty thousand degrees Fahrenheit, five times hotter than the surface of the Sun.
Day one, you most likely live. About ten percent of people struck are killed, says the weather service. Ninety percent survive.
But survive does not mean fine. Lightning mostly injures the nervous system, and scans often look normal.
Survivors report slower reactions, memory trouble, and chronic pain. Now picture day three hundred.
Here is the honest part. Nobody has tested daily strikes, and nothing says a body could adapt.
The record is Roy Sullivan, a Virginia park ranger. Guinness counts seven strikes, from nineteen forty-two to nineteen seventy-seven.
One hit him in his truck. One in his garden. One on a fishing boat. His hair caught fire twice.
Your odds of even one strike are about one in fifteen thousand. If you hear thunder, go inside.
