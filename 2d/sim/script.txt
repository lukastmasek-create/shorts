What if you were the only real human? Philosophers call that idea solipsism.
It comes from the problem of other minds. You only ever experience your own mind. Everyone else, you infer from behavior.
The classic answer is analogy. Others have bodies like yours, and act like you. So they probably have minds too. Critics point out it rests on one case. Yours.
In two thousand three, philosopher Nick Bostrom added a twist. He argued that at least one of three things is true. Civilizations die out. Or they never run simulations. Or we are probably in one. He never claimed which.
The feeling of being watched is real, though. In a famous experiment, students wore an embarrassing shirt. They guessed about half the room noticed. About a quarter did. Psychologists call it the spotlight effect.
In rare cases the belief becomes a delusion. Doctors have described the Truman Show delusion. Patients who believe their life is a staged show. It is not an official diagnosis.
So no experiment settles it. There is no proof either way.
But one result is solid. In a two thousand eight study in Science, people who spent money on others reported more happiness than people who spent it on themselves.
So real or simulated, giving still feels good. Be kind.
