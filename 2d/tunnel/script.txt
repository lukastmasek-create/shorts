What if you dropped a ball into a tunnel straight through the Earth?
The deepest hole ever drilled, in Russia, is twelve kilometers. Barely two tenths of one percent of the way.
Now imagine a finished tunnel, with no air. You let go, and gravity speeds the ball up.
Gravity fades as you go deeper, and at the center it is zero. But the ball is already moving, so it shoots through.
In a simple uniform-Earth model, it peaks near eight kilometers a second.
It reaches the far side in about forty-two minutes. Real Earth is denser at the core, so a 2015 study says thirty-eight.
In that model, any straight tunnel takes forty-two minutes, even a short one.
At the far end, it stops. Then it falls back. Without friction, it swings forever.
In reality, Earth spins, so the ball would hit the wall. And air would slowly settle it at the center.
Forty-two minutes down, forty-two minutes back. Same fall, forever.
