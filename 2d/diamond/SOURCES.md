# DiamondShort (#4 "Planet Made Entirely of Diamond") — fact check
Checked by web search 2026-11-15 (access date for all URLs). Rows marked *secondary* rest on news/encyclopedia summaries, not the primary paper. "A planet made entirely of diamond" is a what-if: no known planet is confirmed to be made of diamond. Money values for any diamond planet are NOT sourced (illustration only, do not show numbers).

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|

| In such a planet the outer layer could be graphite, turning to diamond a few km down under pressure | Scientific American, "Earth-Like Planets May Be Made of Carbon" https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/a-large-lump-of-coal/ | *secondary*; "few km" is press paraphrase of the model |
| 2012: model suggested 55 Cancri e could be carbon-rich, with at least ~1/3 of its mass (~3 Earth masses) diamond | Madhusudhan, Lee & Mousis, "A Possible Carbon-rich Interior in Super-Earth 55 Cancri e", ApJL 759:L40 (2012); Yale News 2012-10-11 https://news.yale.edu/2012/10/11/nearby-super-earth-likely-diamond-planet | "possible"/"likely"; authors asked for atmosphere follow-up to confirm |

| 55 Cancri e dayside ~1540 °C measured (~2200 °C expected without atmosphere) | Hu et al. 2024 (above); Inverse https://www.inverse.com/science/jwst-finds-a-lava-planet-with-a-carbon-dioxide-atmosphere | *secondary* for the °C numbers |

| 2004: white dwarf BPM 37093 ("Lucy"), ~50 ly away in Centaurus, has a crystallized carbon core — "the galaxy's largest diamond", ~10 billion trillion trillion (10^34) carats | Metcalfe, Montgomery & Kanaan, ApJ 605:L133 (2004); CfA release via ScienceDaily 2004-02-14 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2004/02/040214080728.htm | a dead star, not a planet; crystallized fraction ~90% (2004) vs 32–82% in other work |

| Diamond conducts heat ~2000 W/m·K (≈5x copper) | U. Bristol (above) | ranges 900–2320 quoted; "5x copper" is arithmetic vs Cu ~400 W/m·K |
| Diamond density ~3.5 g/cm3 | Wikipedia "Diamond" (via mirror) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond | *secondary*; pure ≈3.52 |
| Diamond burns: ignites ~720–800 °C in oxygen, ~850–1000 °C in air; product CO2 (Lavoisier 1772) | Wikipedia "Diamond" (above) | *secondary*; oxidation starts ~700 °C in air. So in an oxygen atmosphere a hot diamond world would burn; in no-oxygen it would not |

## Not verified / do not use
- Any $ value of 55 Cancri e or another "diamond planet" (popular "$26.9 nonillion" figure was not checked; illustration only).
- "55 Cancri e is made of diamond" as fact — superseded by Teske 2013 and Hu 2024.
- Exact surface texture of a carbon planet (graphite / tar / lava) — models only.
