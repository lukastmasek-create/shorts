What if you could see the fourth dimension? Not time. A fourth direction of space.
Your eyes only capture flat images. Your brain builds the third dimension from them.
In eighteen eighty four, Edwin Abbott imagined Flatland. A sphere crossing a flat world looks like a circle that grows, then shrinks. Only slices.
A fourth dimension would work the same way. You would watch three dimensional slices of a four dimensional object appear, change, and vanish.
Take the tesseract, the four dimensional cube. It has eight cubes, twenty four squares, thirty two edges, and sixteen corners.
By the same geometry, you could see inside a closed box. And inside a person.
Knots would stop working. In four dimensions, a loop of string cannot stay knotted.
Could a brain learn this? In a two thousand nine study, people explored four dimensional shapes in virtual reality. With basic geometry, they learned to judge lengths and angles. Roughly.
Physics already uses four dimensions. Three of space, and one of time, as Minkowski showed in nineteen oh eight. Whether space has a hidden fourth direction is still an open question.
The math is real. Nobody has seen it.
