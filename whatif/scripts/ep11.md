# Episode 11 — What if the world forgot one word each day?

Hook: **WHAT IF THE WORLD FORGOT ONE WORD EVERY DAY?**  
Length: 93.5 s (2805 frames @ 30 fps), 8 scenes

## Scene 1 · 0.0–11.9 s
What if the world forgot one word every day? Monday, nobody can say cheese. The sign above the shop flickers, the letters melt, and everyone just points at the sandwich.

## Scene 2 · 11.9–23.1 s
Tuesday, it's the word umbrella. Wednesday, bicycle. You invent clumsy replacements, like rain hat and two-wheel horse. Dictionaries shrink a little every morning, like autumn trees losing leaves.

## Scene 3 · 23.1–34.3 s
By week two, people start talking in gestures. Waiters mime orders, teachers draw pictures, and street signs become tiny comic strips. Honestly, it's quieter, and kind of beautiful.

## Scene 4 · 34.3–46.6 s
By day one hundred, the big words start vanishing too. Tomorrow. Sorry. Friend. Nobody can say them, so people hold doors, share umbrellas, and bring soup instead. Actions speak louder anyway.

## Scene 5 · 46.6–57.8 s
Scientists would call this impossible, since words don't vanish overnight. In this thought experiment, though, language slowly empties like a bathtub, and you can almost hear the gurgle.

## Scene 6 · 57.8–69.3 s
Day two hundred. The word love disappears. Couples stare at each other, completely stuck. Then, without a word, he hands her an apple, and she hands him her scarf.

## Scene 7 · 69.3–81.6 s
Here's the twist. When the dictionary is nearly empty, people invent new words together. Gloop means happy rain. Fizz means a hug from far away. Language is never gone for long.

## Scene 8 · 81.6–93.5 s
So every morning, the town gathers to invent tomorrow's word together. Today's winner is a tiny kid, who points at the sunrise and says: wow. Everyone agrees. It means everything.
