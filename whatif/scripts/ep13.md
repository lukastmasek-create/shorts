# Episode 13 — What if a second sun rose at midnight?

Hook: **WHAT IF A SECOND SUN ROSE AT MIDNIGHT?**  
Length: 98.3 s (2949 frames @ 30 fps), 8 scenes

## Scene 1 · 0.0–13.0 s
What if a second sun rose at midnight? The clock chimes twelve. The streets are dark and quiet. Then, over the rooftops, a new glow pushes up like someone turning on the world.

## Scene 2 · 13.0–24.9 s
Birds panic. Roosters shout good morning at the moon. Flowers open in the dark, and sleepy people in pajamas drift outside, squinting at the brightest midnight they have ever seen.

## Scene 3 · 24.9–37.2 s
Then the town adapts. Picnics at midnight. Skateboarders at one in the morning. Shops stay open all night, and the owls start a support group for people who now work days.

## Scene 4 · 37.2–50.6 s
But the nights stay too short. Without darkness, nobody sleeps. People nap in cafes, on buses, and in trees. The city is bright and cheerful, but the whole place is also very, very yawny.

## Scene 5 · 50.6–61.8 s
Astronomers shrug. A real second sun would roast the planet, so in this thought experiment, it's a cool, gentle star, more like a friendly nightlight than a furnace.

## Scene 6 · 61.8–74.1 s
Everyone gets double shadows, too. One slides left, one slides right, and they cross like scissors every noon and midnight. Kids spend whole afternoons trying to step on both at once.

## Scene 7 · 74.1–85.6 s
Here's the twist. The second sun doesn't rise every night. It only rises when someone, somewhere, really needs light. Tonight it's for a little girl afraid of the dark.

## Scene 8 · 85.6–98.3 s
So the second sun rises, soft and silver, and the girl falls asleep smiling. Somewhere a rooster mutters, wrong again. And the whole sleepy town sighs: we'll see you tomorrow, little sun.
