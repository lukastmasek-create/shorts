# Episode 04 — What if the Moon moved closer each night?

Hook: **WHAT IF THE MOON MOVED CLOSER EVERY NIGHT?**  
Length: 87.9 s (2637 frames @ 30 fps), 8 scenes

## Scene 1 · 0.0–12.3 s
What if the Moon moved closer every night? Tonight it looks normal. Tomorrow it's a little bigger. By the weekend, kids are using it as a nightlight, and nobody is sleeping.

## Scene 2 · 12.3–22.4 s
On night three, the tides get serious. The sea creeps up the beach, sandcastles surrender, and a very confused crab wonders who moved the shoreline.

## Scene 3 · 22.4–32.5 s
Night five. The moon fills half the sky. Waves roll through the streets, cars bob like bath toys, and the pigeons file a formal complaint.

## Scene 4 · 32.5–43.3 s
Night seven. Scientists say the real tidal pull would rip oceans upward and shake continents. In this thought experiment, the whole planet trembles like a nervous jelly.

## Scene 5 · 43.3–53.7 s
Then comes the Roche limit, where gravity tears the Moon apart. It cracks, wobbles, and bursts into ten thousand glowing pebbles that scatter across the dark.

## Scene 6 · 53.7–64.1 s
The pebbles don't fall. They spread into a glittering ribbon around the Earth, orbiting in perfect circles, like a necklace someone dropped across the whole sky.

## Scene 7 · 64.1–75.6 s
Twist: the Earth gets rings, like Saturn. At night they glow across the whole sky, bright enough to read by. The planet that lost its Moon found a crown.

## Scene 8 · 75.6–87.9 s
Night thirty. The kids lie on the roof, counting glittering pebbles that drift like slow fireflies. The Moon is gone, but the sky has never looked so close, or so kind.
