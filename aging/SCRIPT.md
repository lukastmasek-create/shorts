# What If Your Body Stopped Aging, But Your Brain Kept Aging Normally?
(Assumption: body frozen at age 19. Change if you meant something else.)

## Facts (real)
- Brain volume shrinks about 5% per decade after age 40, faster after 70.
- Processing speed and short-term memory start slowly declining in the late 20s to 30s.
- Vocabulary and general knowledge (crystallized intelligence) keep growing into the 60s.
- Prefrontal cortex (judgment, impulse control) finishes maturing around age 25.
- Wrinkles, grey hair, joint wear come from the body; none of them are caused by the brain.

## Voice-over (~225 words, ~61 s at 3.7 w/s). English, one steady tone.

HOOK
You will stay nineteen forever. Your body, anyway. Your brain did not get that deal.

SETTING
Year one is perfect. No wrinkles. No bad knees. Hair stays thick.

ACTION
At thirty, your friends start looking tired. You still get carded.
At fifty, your friends have grey hair. A bouncer asks if you are old enough to be here.
At seventy, you can still sprint. You just forgot why you started running.
At ninety, your body could win a race. Your brain is still looking for the starting line.

SNAP
Then one morning you are standing in the kitchen. You have no idea why.

DEADPAN
Luckily, you will never need a cane.
Unluckily, you will need a note on the fridge that says: this is your fridge.

EXPLANATION
Here is the thing. After forty, your brain shrinks about five percent every decade.
Thinking gets slower. Memory gets patchy.
But the vocabulary and the stories keep growing for decades.
It is a brand new sports car, driven by a very wise, very confused grandpa.

CONSEQUENCE
Everyone treats you like a teenager. Nobody listens to your advice.
You have eighty years of memories, and a face that gets you a kids' menu.

CALLBACK TWIST
So you do the only thing you can. You sit on a bench, look young, and say:
I remember when this was all a field.
And for once, nobody can prove you wrong.
